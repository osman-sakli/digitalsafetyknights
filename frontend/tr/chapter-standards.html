🛡️ Chapter Standards & Safeguarding

What we require of anyone who runs a chapter in our name, how we check it, and what ends a chapter. Written down so that we can be held to it too.

A chapter carries our name in front of children. That is the whole reason this page exists. Our materials are free and public and we want them used by anyone, anywhere, with no permission from us — but calling yourself Digital Safety Knights is a different thing, and it is not automatic.

We say yes to people who are already accountable to someone else for children's safety. We say no to people who are not, however good their intentions, and we have said no before.

1 · The rules that are not negotiable

All eight apply from the first session. There is no partial version and no trial exemption.

  1. You are 18 or over.Under-18s are welcome and wanted — through Knight Council, our teen programme, where an adult is responsible. Not as a chapter lead.
  2. You never work with children alone, and never as a private individual.Every session happens through a host — a school, a school club, a library, an NGO, a faith group, a youth organisation — that has its own child-protection policy and its own responsible adult present in the room. We do not approve anyone who wants to gather children themselves.
  3. You give us a verifiable affiliation and one reference we can contact.A head teacher, an organisation director, a supervisor. Someone whose responsibility for those children predates us, and who will answer an email from a stranger about you.
  4. Whatever vetting your country and your host require is done by them, before you start.A background check, a police certificate, a safeguarding clearance — whatever applies where you are. We do not perform these checks and we will never pretend our approval is one.
  5. No one-to-one contact with a child, ever.No private messages, no personal phone number, no personal social account used for chapter work, no adding a child as a friend or follower. Communication goes through the host organisation. Photographs of children only under the host's own consent process, never yours.
  6. A disclosure goes to the host's safeguarding lead immediately.And to the local authority wherever the law requires it. You do not investigate, you do not gather evidence, you do not contact a family yourself, and you do not wait to tell us first. Tell us afterwards.
  7. Nothing is ever charged, collected or fundraised in our name.No fees to families or schools, no sponsorships, no donations, no selling anything. Everything a chapter delivers is free. If money needs to exist, it happens between the host organisation and its own funders, with no reference to us.
  8. Translate and localise freely; do not change the safety content.Please adapt our material to your country's platforms, laws and language — that is most of the value of a chapter. But the safety guidance itself, and the age banding that decides what is said to a six-year-old versus a sixteen-year-old, stay as they are. Send changes back to us and we will publish them properly, credited.

2 · What we give, and what we do not

We give

  • Every slide deck, facilitator guide, quiz, printable and film we have, free
  • The full certification programme — four age tracks, slides, exams, certificates
  • A named person on our team you can email and get an answer from
  • Review of anything you localise, and publication with your credit on it
  • A listing on our chapters page once you are active

We do not give

  • Money, of any kind, in any direction
  • Devices, printing, venues or travel
  • Insurance, legal cover or indemnity
  • Employment, a contract, or a title you can put on a CV as a job
  • A background check, or any kind of vetting of you that a host could rely on
  • Exclusivity — several chapters can exist in one country

3 · How we actually verify someone

Every step happens with a real person, and the whole thing usually takes two to three weeks. We are not fast and we are not sorry about it.

  1. The form. The application form is the only intake route. If you email us instead we will ask you to fill it in, because an application that is not recorded cannot be reviewed consistently.
  2. A read, and usually questions. We reply to everyone. Most applications get a handful of specific questions back before anything else.
  3. A video call. With your camera on. This is not a test of your English or your confidence; it is us wanting to meet the person who will be in a room with children.
  4. We contact your reference. Directly, ourselves, and we tell you we are doing it.
  5. You confirm the eight rules in writing. By email, in your own words, not a tick-box.
  6. A provisional first term. Your first sessions run with the host organisation's adult present and a short note back to us afterwards. After that, you are a chapter and you are listed.

4 · What ends a chapter

Immediately, publicly, and without notice

  • Working with children outside a host organisation, or without its responsible adult present.
  • Any one-to-one contact with a child.
  • Charging, collecting or fundraising money in our name.
  • Changing the safety content, or teaching a younger age group material written for an older one.
  • A disclosure that was not passed to the host's safeguarding lead.
  • Using the chapter to recruit for, sell, or promote anything else — a business, a candidate, a congregation.

Reporting a concern about a chapter: use our contact form, which goes straight to our team, or write to team@digitalsafetyknights.org. You do not need evidence and you do not need to give your name. We would much rather look into ten things that turn out to be nothing.

The honest part

Digital Safety Knights is a father and a teenage daughter with a student club. We cannot supervise what happens in a classroom in another country, and we are not going to claim we can. We have no staff, no field officers, no capacity to audit a session in Kampala or Karachi.

That limitation is exactly why rule 2 is absolute rather than a preference. We are not the safeguard — the host organisation is, with its own policy, its own vetting and its own adult in the room. Our job is to refuse to lend our name where that structure does not already exist.

If this page reads as cautious for an organisation that gives everything else away for nothing, that is deliberate. The materials cost us nothing to share and we share them with anyone. Our name in front of a child is the one thing we are careful with.
