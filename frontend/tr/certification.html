How it works

Four steps, two weeks, one certificate

The same shape for every age group. Only the length, the language and the content change.

1

Pick a track

Choose the age band. A class sits one track together; a parent evening sits the Guardian track.

2

Session 1 — what happens

The threats, named precisely and age-appropriately. One hour, live, with the room talking back.

3

Session 2 — what to do

A week later. Hands-on: settings changed, protocols rehearsed, the first sentence to an adult scripted.

4

The exam, then the certificate

Open book, 80% to pass, unlimited free retakes. Pass and the certificate is issued with your name on it.

The four tracks

The same course, pitched four ways

A 2-hour block is right for a teenager and wrong for a seven-year-old, so the younger tracks are shorter and the exam is shorter with them. What a child is told also changes by band: the Sentinel track names sextortion and the law outright, the Knight track stops at “if anyone ever asks you for a photo, that is the moment you tell a grown-up”, and the Squire track never goes near it.

🗡️

Squire

Ages 6–9 · lower elementary
2 × 30 minutes · 12-question exam

Secrets, strangers and shiny prizes. Taught with hands up and voices out — an interaction every three minutes, because that is how this age learns.

  • Your name, school and address stay offline
  • “Free” plus “hurry” is always a trap
  • “Keep it a secret from your parents” is always wrong
  • Never meet someone from a game in real life
  • Telling a grown-up is never the wrong answer

Knight

Ages 10–13 · upper elementary & middle
2 × 45 minutes · 18-question exam

The Roblox, Discord and first-phone years. The tricks get named, and in Session 2 the settings get changed live on their own devices.

  • Grooming as a four-stage pattern you can name
  • AI that sounds certain and is wrong · voice cloning
  • Group-chat bullying, and what a bystander does

Sentinel

2 × 60 minutes · 25-question exam

The full landscape, no euphemism. This is the track built for the age group the data says is most targeted — and it treats them as competent, not careless.

Parents, carers & educators

Before you ask

The questions schools actually send us

Why is it free? What is the catch?

There is no catch and nothing held back for a paying tier, because there is no paying tier. We are a nonprofit, the cost to us is our own time, and the materials cost nothing to share. The only money anywhere near this is donations, which are voluntary and which we do not ask a school for.

What is the difference between a self-issued and a verified certificate?

A self-issued certificate records that the exam was passed in that student's browser. We did not watch them sit it, so we cannot vouch for who did. A verified certificate comes from a roster you gave us, a sitting your teacher proctored, and a reference we can confirm if someone asks. Both are real; only one is something a district can rely on.

Can we just run the course ourselves?

Yes, and you do not need to tell us. Every deck and facilitator guide is published with the full run sheet and every presenter note. Print it, share it, translate it, adapt it for your room. If you want the exam too, it is already live. We would rather a teacher in a country we will never visit runs this next Tuesday than wait for us.

Is any student data collected?

Not by the self-serve route. The exam runs entirely in the browser: the name, the answers and the result never leave the device, there is no account and there is no server. If you ask for verified certificates we necessarily hold a roster of names so they can be issued and confirmed — nothing else, never shared, and deleted at the end of the school year unless you ask us to keep it.

How much is each session, really? Our timetable is 45 minutes.

The Knight track is built for exactly that — 2 × 45 minutes. The Sentinel and Guardian tracks are 60 but will compress to 45 if you tell us in advance which segment to drop; the facilitator guide marks the timings so you can see what gets cut. The Squire track is 2 × 30, because a 60-minute online session with six-year-olds does not work and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

Who teaches it?

Our team — most often Ayşe Saklı, our Club Leader and Co-Founder. See the team page for who's who. Students aged 13–17 who want to keep going after the course can apply to Knight Council.

What if a student discloses something during a session?

It happens, and the guides are written for it. We never ask a student to describe their own experience in front of a group, we keep a private channel open throughout, and we stay on the call for ten minutes after the end — in our sessions disclosures come after the end far more often than during. Anything that needs escalating goes to your designated safeguarding lead immediately, and we do not run an investigation ourselves.

Is the certificate an accredited qualification?

No, and we will not claim otherwise. It is a certificate of completion from a nonprofit, not a credential from an awarding body. It belongs on a classroom wall, in a portfolio, or in a student file as evidence that the training happened. If anyone tells you a two-hour online course is an accredited qualification, be suspicious of them.
